01 — Overview
Core Message
Spine
The advice to say yes is not wrong. For a significant stretch of most careers, it is exactly right — it builds visibility, opens doors, and puts you in rooms you wouldn't otherwise reach. The problem is that nobody tells you when it stops being the right advice. This episode is a diagnosis of what happens when you keep using an operating system that has quietly become a liability: the cost of the miscalculated yes, the fear underneath the automatic yes, and the social cost of the moment you start saying no. The gender dimension is real and the episode names it — but the fundamental insight is not exclusively female. It is about outgrowing advice without realising you have outgrown it.
The Distinction — What This Episode Says That Has Not Been Said
Most content on this topic addresses people who are overwhelmed and need permission to push back. This episode addresses people who are strategic but using the wrong map. The advice was not wrong — it was right for a different season. The failure is systemic: nobody updates the instructions. Do not market this as another "women need to set boundaries" episode. That is precisely what the episode argues against.
The Cost — What It Costs a Leader Who Doesn't Hear This
She stays busy past the point where busy serves her. She says yes to things that consume her time, her energy, and her reputation without advancing where she is trying to go. In the most costly version: she spends her career being indispensable to everyone except herself.
This Episode Is Not About
Burnout. Work-life balance. Being overwhelmed. Setting boundaries as self-care. This is not a wellness episode. It is a career infrastructure episode for people who are already senior and already strategic — but may be using a map that no longer fits the territory.

05 — Show Notes
Opening Line
The advice to say yes is not wrong. It built careers. It opened doors. Nobody told us it had a season.
Episode Description
For years, the advice was simple: say yes. Say yes to the project, the committee, the stretch assignment, the last-minute trip. It was good advice — for a season. In this episode, Uche Ofodile looks honestly at what happens when you keep using that operating system long after the conditions around you have changed, and why nobody comes back to update the instructions.
Key Themes
— The advice that gets you into the room is not always the advice that helps you lead well once you are there.
— The difference between a good opportunity and a good opportunity for you.
— The yes that comes from strategy versus the yes that comes from fear.
— What happens when people have become accustomed to your yes — and you start saying no.
— The certainty that builds over time, and why it takes longer than it should.
