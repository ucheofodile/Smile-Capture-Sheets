What Do I Actually Owe Another Woman?

Solo · Uche Ofodile · Floor: Presence (primary), Support · Recorded October 2026 · Raw length 13:40, edited target about 11 minutes
← All capture sheets
1. Overview
Core Message
Spine
A woman Uche gave conditional promotion feedback to changed her WhatsApp picture to say there is a special place in hell for women who don't support other women. The episode answers the question it left her with: what does a woman in power actually owe the women coming behind her? Not an automatic yes. She owes the fight in the rooms they are not in, the truth about what is waiting for them there, and the honesty to keep asking whether her own preparation has become part of the unfairness.
The Distinction
It rejects both familiar positions: "women don't support women" and "women should support women no matter what." The original idea is what a sponsor keeps after a woman gets the role. Uche heard the doubts the woman never did, and that knowledge shapes how hard she pushes her. The Ghana story shows why: women are judged as a group, so every disagreement risks becoming a story about women.
Operating Principle
Prepare women rigorously for the room they are walking into, but don't add your own extra margin of error on top of the one the world already imposes.
The Cost
A leader who confuses support with agreement leaves women unprepared. A leader who confuses preparation with a higher bar becomes another part of the unfairness she is trying to protect them from.
Audience Promise
Senior women who have sponsored others will recognise the knowledge they keep about the rooms. Women who once had a tough woman boss may see her differently, and may also recognise where she went too far.
This Episode Is Not About
Do not market it as
  • Another "women supporting women" conversation. That is the frame it argues against.
  • A defence of tough women bosses. Uche questions her own toughness.
  • The woman with the WhatsApp picture. She is not the villain and must not be identifiable.
  • A call to action. The episode ends unresolved on purpose.

What Landed Better Than the Script
The natural reactions. "How far?" in the opening is funnier and more specific than the scripted "seriously?" "Are we looking at the same document?" is sharper than the scripted line. Reconnecting with the Ghana colleagues at the same book event ties the Ghana story back to the opening naturally.
Where the Recording Diverged, Worth Keeping
The expanded ending. Each of the three things she owes is now explained in a sentence or two, including "There is no need for you to repeat mistakes I may have made in my own journey."
Where the Recording Diverged, Must Fix
Around 10:50: "I want you to be ready because you cannot afford to get anything wrong." As recorded, this endorses the idea the episode questions. It needs a pickup line (see Tolu tab).

2. Tolu
Edit Instructions
Overall Instruction
The whole episode answers one question: what do I owe another woman? Protect that thread. Cut the bridges and repeats where Uche loses it, and keep the reactions where she sounds most like herself.
Tone to Preserve
Lightly irritated and funny at the start, warm and reflective by the end. Don't smooth out the humour ("How far?", "nobody applied to attend this school"). The last section should feel slower and more uncertain than the first.
Edit for Three Questions
  1. Does every section move the answer to "what do I owe her?" forward?
  2. Would a new listener follow it without the script?
  3. Does the ending stay open, or has something resolved it?
Restart Markers
"Strawberry," used seven times. Approximate raw positions: 00:30 (twice), 03:20, 04:00, 05:20, 05:45, 10:40. Take the clean line after each one.
Pickup Required Before Final Edit
Around 10:50, replace "And someone somehow. I want you to be ready because you cannot afford to get anything wrong" with Uche's pickup recording: "And somewhere in there, 'I want you ready' becomes 'you cannot afford to get anything wrong.'" Do not finalise the edit without it.
Pacing Notes
The Ghana section runs long because of three attempts at the same line. Tighten it hard. Give the room story (06:15 to 08:20) space to breathe. Leave a beat before "Now, here's the part I'm not always very certain about" and before "What do I owe another woman?"
Sections
Open (00:00 to 01:00)
First line: "So I've been having these book events recently..." Remove the restarts around 00:30. "I call cap" is pending Uche's decision; keep it in the rough cut.
The Accusation (01:00 to 02:30)
Keep "How far? Why am I, why am I here? Is this about me?" in full. Keep it fast.
Not an Automatic Yes (02:30 to 03:50)
Trim the repeated "What did she think I owed her" so the question is asked once, cleanly. Cut "Oof. This honestly makes me very annoyed when I hear women say that" if it reads as unclear about what "that" refers to.
Ghana (03:50 to 06:15)
Correct "four or five of us" to "four of us" if Uche confirms the number. Use the last clean version of the repeated line: "it was the same way that we felt people could potentially look at us all those years ago." Cut the bridge "So coming back to this, the fact that there's always a narrative around women leadership..." and go straight to "Sitting in a room many years ago."
The Room (06:15 to 08:30)
This is the heart of the episode. Trim "Because she's gotten the job, of course. I've helped push, as I should, because she was the right candidate" to keep momentum.
School of Uche (08:30 to 09:50)
Cut "And that's why." at 09:25. Keep the CV line intact.
The Doubt (09:50 to 11:45)
Cut "It's not that she's not supporting you... think about it from that point of view" so "taking your future most seriously" lands on its own. Insert the pickup line around 10:50.
Close (11:45 to end)
End the content on "The third one I'm still working on." Then go straight to "Thank you, as always, for listening." Cut "And if you're a woman boss, you know what to do... They need our support" and "All righty." "Go to bat for you" is pending Uche's decision.

Episode Description
After Uche gave a woman a clear path to promotion, with conditions, the woman changed her WhatsApp picture to say there is a special place in hell for women who don't support other women. Uche rejects both "women don't support women" and the idea that support should be automatic. She asks what a woman in power really owes the women coming behind her, and whether preparing them for unfair rooms can quietly become part of the unfairness.
Key Themes
  • Why support is not an automatic yes
  • How women are judged as a group, not as individuals
  • What a sponsor knows that the woman she sponsored doesn't
  • The line between rigorous preparation and a higher standard
