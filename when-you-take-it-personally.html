When You Take It Personally

Thumbnail Line (working direction): Everybody is not thinking about you.
Uche Ofodile · The Basement · The Smile Collective
Core Message
Spine — From the Recording
There is a huge difference between being harmed by a decision and being the reason for it. Sometimes people are being deliberate — that is real and should not be minimised. But sometimes people are simply solving their own problem and you become collateral damage. The episode is not about pretending everything is fine. It is about what happens when you misread collateral damage as a personal attack — and the cost of that misreading when you are leading people.
Three-Movement Structure — For Editor Reference
Movement 1 — Coachee story: what personalisation does. She was harmed by the decision. She was not the reason for it. The distinction between those two things is what the episode is built around. Movement 2 — Monrovia: what becomes possible when you stop reacting. Not a strategy session. Just stopping. And out of that stopping came the book, the podcast, a different way of thinking about leadership. Movement 3 — Business story: why this matters even more when you lead. At leadership level, personalisation does not just affect how you feel. It enters your judgment. It starts making decisions. That is the leadership payoff of the episode and what stops it from being generic advice.
The Distinction
This episode is not about emotional detachment or resilience. It is not an "everything is fine" conversation — Uche says that explicitly at 02:44. It is about what happens when you let the interpretation enter your judgment before you have examined it. Sometimes it is personal — the episode says so explicitly. The leadership problem is not the feeling. It is acting from the interpretation before you have tested whether it is true.
Operating Principle — From the Recording at 18:58
Pause before reacting. The practical check Uche names in the recording: "Is this decision being made out of anger or is this the right decision for the business? Am I reacting because I'm frustrated or am I reacting in a way that's positive for my team?" That is the tool in this episode. Not a generic reframe — a leadership-specific check on whether frustration has entered the decision.
The Cost — Exact Words from Recording at 21:16
You stay in fights long after they stop serving the business. You refuse conversations you should have had. You spend energy proving a point instead of solving the next problem. This is the episode's leadership payoff. It answers why a leader should care about this beyond the emotional dimension. Give it prominence.

Show Notes
Opening Line
There is a huge difference between being harmed by a decision and being the reason for it. Most of us collapse those two things — and it costs us more than we realise.
Episode Description
In this episode, Uche talks about what happens when you misread a difficult situation as a personal attack — and what it costs you strategically when you do. A coaching client who almost resigned over a promotion she didn't get. Three days sitting by the ocean in Monrovia. A business sale that felt like a personal betrayal. And what changed when she finally understood that being harmed by a decision is not the same as being the reason for it.
Key Themes
The difference between being harmed by a decision and being the reason for it. How personalisation changes how we respond — and eventually how we lead. What becomes possible when you stop letting frustration decide what comes next. The basement: how unprocessed hurt enters strategy without you realising. The pause — and why it is a leadership discipline, not a passive response.

Saturday / Sunday — Reflective Follow-Up
The most honest line I said in this week's episode wasn't in the script. It came out in the recording and I am still sitting with it. "Why are you there collecting evidence for your own hurts?"
