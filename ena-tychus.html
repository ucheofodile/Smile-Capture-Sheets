Your Body Doesn't Know You're the CEO
Guest — Dr. Ena Tychus, Founder & CEO, GetMed Concierge · Recorded [CONFIRM DATE]
Core Message
Spine
Senior women build infrastructure around every part of their lives except their bodies, and wait until something forces the issue before paying attention. Dr. Ena Tychus had already decided to leave her senior leadership role at Kaiser when her father's metastatic cancer diagnosis landed on her literal last day there, a discovery that gave the decision profound meaning and let her spend his final months with him. She went on to found a concierge medicine practice in Nigeria, and makes the case that health needs the same deliberate planning as any other part of a woman's life, before the crisis, not after.
The Distinction
Not a menopause explainer or a wellness-trend roundup. The founder story carries real emotional weight (her father's diagnosis landing on her literal last day at Kaiser, her husband telling her God was going to give her a business, the "USB download" vision), and both host and guest speak about menopause and HRT with unusual directness rather than the hedged, general version of this conversation. GLP-1s come up only briefly at the very end, deliberately deferred rather than discussed.
Operating Principle
Health infrastructure means having an actual plan, and someone who knows your whole picture, before something goes wrong, not reacting symptom by symptom.
The Cost
A leader who doesn't build this can keep performing for a long time while her health quietly deteriorates, waiting until her body forces the question rather than managing her health deliberately beforehand.

Episode Description
This episode is about the things senior women ask quietly: what years of stress actually do to the body, what's happening with menopause and HRT, which tests actually matter, and why we're better at taking care of everyone else than ourselves. Dr. Ena Tychus, who had already decided to leave her senior leadership role at Kaiser Permanente when her father's cancer diagnosis landed on her literal last day there, brings both the medical answers and an extraordinary personal story about building a concierge medicine practice in Nigeria afterward.
Key Themes
What chronic stress actually does to the body. Menopause and HRT without the hedging. What senior women carry privately and rarely say out loud. Building real health infrastructure, not just doing a lot of health things. Why we take care of everyone except ourselves. Leaving a secure career for an unclear calling.
